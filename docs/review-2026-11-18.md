# Contract review, 2026-11-18

Scope: `crates/neap/src/{contract,exchange,policy,model}.rs` and how they call the vendored engine. The engine's
own math was treated as verified. Off-chain services and the frontend were reviewed in other threads.

Method: six independent reviewers (accounting and custody, risk math, oracle and orders, NEAR platform, access and
upgrades, test coverage). Each wrote tests that assert the correct behaviour, so they failed before the fix. Several
findings were reached independently by two to four reviewers, which is the cross-check. Raw notes with file and
line references are in `docs/review-2026-11-18/`.

Not run: Kani, the wasm build and the sandbox tests. None of the three tools is installed in the review
environment, so the security gate in `AGENTS.md` is only partly met. Run them before merging.

## 2. Open findings (not fixed, no decision needed, need engineering)

**F-1. `Engine(Stale)` on a reduce-only close (severity unknown).** The fuzz saw a reduce-only close by the only
positioned trader refused with `Engine(Stale)` after a partial close and several price walks. Four hand-written
variants did not reproduce it. If real, a trader can be unable to close. Needs a minimised reproduction.

**F-2. LP pool profit is never converted (high).** Pool losses hit pool capital at once; pool gains stay as engine
`pnl`. `lp_redeem` pays the lower NAV bound, which excludes them, and nothing can call `convert_pnl` for the pool.
A sole LP got back exactly 1,000,000 USDC after the pool earned about 300; after the last LP left, a 1,000 USDC
round trip returned 769 USDC. A fix was attempted here (refresh the pool, then convert, on every price push and
crank) and backed out: on a flat pool the engine answered `Stale` and then `LockActive` on every one of 101 blocks
and never converted. This is the same mechanism as F-1 in `docs/contract.md` (unconverted profit loses its backing
after the horizon) and needs someone who knows the engine's warmup and backing rules. Tests:
`poc_accounting_lp_profit` (ignored).

**F-3. Verification claims that do not hold.** "Custody Kani-proved" covers only the `Custody` struct, not its link
to engine vaults (which finding 1 broke). The README's sandbox flow lists a liquidation the sandbox test no longer
runs. No Kani harness exists for `ema_next`, `dynamic_spread_bps`, `observation_staleness` or `oi_within_cap`; the
LP share proofs use 16-bit domains. `cargo fmt --check` has 95 diffs; clippy with `-D warnings` fails on the engine.

**F-4. Smaller items.** `get_account` and `get_market` return unrefreshed capital, PnL and NAV, so UI equity and
liquidation price go stale as the price moves. No view lists a market's risk parameters or an owner's orders. The
wrapper's `effective_position_q` floors where the engine rounds up. `liquidate`, `claim_fees` and `CreateMarket`
create wallet entries without a storage deposit. `new` is not `#[private]` (deploy and init must be one batch).
An unparsable `ft_on_transfer` message is credited to the wallet instead of refunded. Rounding dust goes to
protocol in code and to insurance in spec invariant 8.

## 3. Proposals for jason

These change economics, the trust model or the architecture. Nothing here was changed. Tests that encode the
recommended answer are in the tree, marked `#[ignore]` with the proposal number.

**P-1. Opens stall unless every positioned account is refreshed each block (critical for launch).**
The engine refuses any position-increasing fill while another account with a position has not been refreshed in
the current block. With one trader this never shows. With two, the second trader's open fails with
`Engine(LockActive)` until the keeper runs `keeper_step` on the first, in the same block, every block the price or
funding moves. Proved here: second trader refused on either side; passes once the first is refreshed. So the cost
of one open is one keeper call per positioned account, and idle holders stall the market. Options: (a) keeper
sweeps all positioned accounts before every fill (works for tens of accounts, not thousands); (b) `execute_order`
takes a bounded list of accounts to refresh in the same call; (c) an engine-level change (separate PR, spec diff,
Kani). Recommend (b) now and (c) after the hackathon.

**P-2. LP 30%, insurance 10% and the 40% liquidation share are stranded (high).** All fees land in the engine's
unbudgeted insurance. Only lister and protocol can claim. The LP ledger only grows and never reaches pool NAV. The
engine covers bad debt only from budgeted insurance, which only `insurance_deposit` creates, so the fee-funded
insurance absorbs nothing: without donations the effective insurance fund is zero. Recommend: on each fill, move
the LP share into pool capital and the insurance share into the budgeted insurance domains.

**P-3. Listing bond and rules (high).** The bond is credited to the lister's withdrawable wallet and can be
withdrawn at once. Market storage (about 12 KB, 0.12 NEAR) is paid by the contract. Risk parameters are not
checked against tier bounds, symbols can be duplicated, and OI caps start uncapped. Recommend: escrow the bond
until the market closes, charge storage in NEAR or from the bond, enforce tier bounds, reject duplicate symbols,
default caps from pool depth.

**P-4. NearPool price source (critical for NearPool markets).** The mark is a 5% EMA of spot pool reserves. After
a 10% spot move the mark moves 0.5%; a trader can open at the lagging mark and close as it catches up. The lister
names the `dex` account with no allowlist, and depth is one self-reported reading at activation. Decimals are
assumed to be 6. Recommend: allowlist of DEX contracts (Ref/Rhea), a time-weighted price with a deviation check
against spot, minimum depth re-checked on every read, decimals in the price source.

**P-5. Mode recovery and exits (high).** No role can loosen a mode. In `PriceUncertain` even closes are refused
while `liquidate` keeps running, so the pauser key can freeze positions for good. `Recovery`, `WindDown` and
`Closed` are unreachable; there is no settlement path. Recommend: owner (later multisig) can restore `Active`
after a delay; closes are always allowed while liquidation is; add a wind-down that settles at a final price.

**P-6. Two-step orders do not stop price picking (high).** The fill is not bound to the first observation after
the request. The requester can execute at any point inside 50 blocks, and "newer" is measured in blocks, so a
price published before the request qualifies. No margin is escrowed, contrary to the spec. Recommend: fill at the
first observation whose `source_ts` is after the request, only the keeper may execute during the first few blocks,
and escrow margin at request.

**P-7. Storage charges (medium).** `request_order` stores 115 bytes for 1 yocto with no per-account limit; 300
orders cost the contract 34.5 KB. Conditional storage deposits are never refunded. There is no `close_account` or
NEP-145, so about 0.1 NEAR per market per trader is locked forever. Changing this alters what the frontend
attaches, so it is listed here. Recommend: charge and refund real storage on orders, cap open orders per account,
add NEP-145.

**P-8. F-1 mitigation cannot work as documented (high).** `convert_pnl` is caller-only and `convert_pnl_for` does
not exist, so the keeper cannot convert for users. Profit from a keeper-filled take-profit is stranded once
`h_max` passes (about 30 s with the repo's parameters). Recommend: add permissionless `convert_pnl_for`, and
convert inside `execute_conditional` and `execute_order` on a close.

**P-9. Pool leverage and insurance of last resort (high).** With caps at 0 one taker can lever the pool to 1/IM: a
1,000 USDC pool took 9,900 notional, and after a 6% move anyone could liquidate the pool itself for a bounty. The
pool's own account is a valid target of `liquidate` and `keeper_step`. Liquidators earn nothing on accounts with no
capital left, which are the ones that create bad debt. Recommend: default caps tied to pool capital (spec section
5 formula), exclude the pool account from `liquidate`, a minimum liquidation reward.

**P-10. Upgrades and emergency stop (medium).** No `migrate`, no versioning of contract state, `Market`, `Order` or
`ConditionalOrder`, and an engine layout change would make every portfolio unreadable. Upgrades are raw redeploys
with a full-access key. There is no global pause. `set_owner` is one step. Recommend: versioned state enums, a
`migrate` gated by the owner, a two-step owner transfer, a contract-wide pause, and no full-access key on mainnet.

**P-11. Funding pays the cap rate for any one-sided book (medium).** The rate is normalised by current OI, so 10
units against a 1M pool pays the full cap (about 1.44% a day with the repo's parameters). The leverage ratchet in
spec section 5 is implemented in `policy.rs` but never called. Recommend: normalise funding by the OI cap or pool
depth, and wire the ratchet in.

**P-12. Oracle trust (medium).** `push_price` is one trusted account with no band and no signature check.
`submit_price` and `sync_tee_keys` do not exist, so `Tee` markets depend on that account. `set_oracle` has no
delay. This matches the phase plan; it is listed so the residual risk is explicit before mainnet.
